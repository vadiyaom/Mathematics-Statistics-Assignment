TASK 2: Swiggy Premium Restaurant Discount - Bayes' Theorem Analysis
================================================================================

1. Problem Scenario:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
On Swiggy, 10% of restaurants are 'premium', and 80% of premium restaurants 
offer a discount, while only 20% of regular restaurants do. If you see a discount, 
use Bayes' theorem in code to find the probability that the restaurant is premium.

Hint: Define prior, likelihood, and evidence in your code with comments.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Definition of Variables and Events:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Let Event H_premium = The restaurant is 'Premium'
- Let Event H_regular = The restaurant is 'Regular' (H_regular = not H_premium)
- Let Event E_discount= The restaurant offers a 'Discount' (Observed Evidence)

Given Probabilities:
1. Prior Probabilities:
   - P(Premium) = 0.10  (10% of restaurants on Swiggy are premium)
   - P(Regular) = 0.90  (90% of restaurants are regular)

2. Likelihood Probabilities:
   - P(Discount | Premium) = 0.80  (80% of premium restaurants offer a discount)
   - P(Discount | Regular) = 0.20  (20% of regular restaurants offer a discount)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Mathematical Derivation:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 1: Compute Total Marginal Evidence P(Discount)
Using the Law of Total Probability:
      P(Discount) = P(Premium) * P(Discount | Premium) + P(Regular) * P(Discount | Regular)
                  = (0.10 * 0.80) + (0.90 * 0.20)
                  = 0.08 + 0.18
                  = 0.26 (or 26% of all restaurants on Swiggy offer a discount)

Step 2: Apply Bayes' Theorem to Find Posterior P(Premium | Discount)
                                  P(Premium) * P(Discount | Premium)
      P(Premium | Discount) = -----------------------------------------
                                             P(Discount)

                                          0.10 * 0.80
                            = -----------------------------------
                                             0.26

                                             0.08
                            = -----------------------------------
                                             0.26

                            = 4 / 13
                            approx 0.30769 (or 30.77%)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Python Implementation (task2_swiggy_bayes.py):
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
```python
# 1. Define Priors
prior_premium = 0.10               # P(Premium)
prior_regular = 1.0 - 0.10         # P(Regular) = 0.90

# 2. Define Likelihoods
likelihood_disc_given_prem = 0.80  # P(Discount | Premium)
likelihood_disc_given_reg  = 0.20  # P(Discount | Regular)

# 3. Calculate Marginal Evidence: P(Discount)
evidence_discount = (prior_premium * likelihood_disc_given_prem) + \
                    (prior_regular * likelihood_disc_given_reg)

# 4. Calculate Posterior using Bayes' Theorem
posterior_premium = (prior_premium * likelihood_disc_given_prem) / evidence_discount

print(f"Total Evidence P(Discount)        : {evidence_discount:.4f} (26.0%)")
print(f"Posterior P(Premium | Discount)    : {posterior_premium:.5f} ({posterior_premium * 100:.2f}%)")
```

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Intuitive Explanation & The Base-Rate Effect:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why is the probability only 30.77% when 80% of premium restaurants give discounts?

Consider a concrete population of 100 Swiggy restaurants:
- 10 are Premium:
  80% of them offer discounts ==> 8 discounted premium restaurants.
- 90 are Regular:
  20% of them offer discounts ==> 18 discounted regular restaurants.

Total restaurants with discounts = 8 + 18 = 26 restaurants.
Among these 26 discounted restaurants:
- Only 8 are Premium.
- 18 are Regular.

Therefore:
P(Premium | Discount) = 8 / 26 = 4 / 13 = **30.77%**.

Conclusion:
Because regular restaurants heavily dominate the market (90% base rate), even a 
small discount rate of 20% among regular restaurants produces more total discounted 
restaurants (18) than the premium group produces (8). 
Observing a discount triples our initial belief from 10% to 30.77%, but it remains 
more likely that the restaurant is regular (~69.23%).
