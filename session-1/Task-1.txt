TASK 1: Daily App Actions and Data Classification
=================================================

Below are 5 daily actions performed on popular apps (Instagram, Zomato, Flipkart), along with the classification of data generated by each action:

1. Action: Rating a delivered food order from 1 to 5 stars on Zomato
   - App: Zomato
   - Data Generated: Star rating score (e.g., 4 stars, 5 stars)
   - Data Type: Numerical (Discrete) / Ordinal Categorical
   - Explanation: It represents a distinct, countable numeric value indicating customer satisfaction level. When used for mathematical computations (like calculating average restaurant ratings), it functions as discrete numerical data. From a survey standpoint, it can also be viewed as ordinal categorical due to ranked satisfaction levels.

2. Action: Liking a post or reel on Instagram
   - App: Instagram
   - Data Generated: Interaction state (Liked / Not Liked)
   - Data Type: Categorical (Binary / Nominal)
   - Explanation: The action generates a binary state (True/False or 1/0) representing user engagement. It reflects a qualitative attribute rather than a measurable quantity. (Note: The aggregated count of total likes on a post across all users becomes Discrete Numerical data).

3. Action: Entering delivery pincode / selecting city on Flipkart
   - App: Flipkart
   - Data Generated: Pincode or City name (e.g., "560001" or "Bangalore")
   - Data Type: Categorical (Nominal)
   - Explanation: Even though a postal pincode consists of numbers, it is a label identifying a geographic delivery zone. Arithmetic operations like addition or finding the average pincode have no meaningful interpretation, making it nominal categorical data.

4. Action: Paying the final order bill amount at checkout on Zomato
   - App: Zomato
   - Data Generated: Transaction amount (e.g., ₹452.50)
   - Data Type: Numerical (Continuous)
   - Explanation: Payment value is a quantitative monetary measurement that can take any fractional value within a range and supports full mathematical operations (mean, variance, totals).

5. Action: Selecting a product size (S, M, L, XL, XXL) for clothing on Flipkart
   - App: Flipkart
   - Data Generated: Garment size category (e.g., "M", "L", "XL")
   - Data Type: Categorical (Ordinal)
   - Explanation: Clothing sizes are qualitative labels that possess a natural, logical order or rank (Small < Medium < Large < XL), but the intervals between sizes cannot be quantified with precise arithmetic differences.
