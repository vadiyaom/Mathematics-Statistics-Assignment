TASK 4: Real-World Scenario Modeling with Normal Distribution
================================================================================

1. Problem Description:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pick any real-world scenario (like delivery times for Zomato orders or playlist 
song durations on Spotify) and explain in 3-4 lines why the normal distribution 
might be a good fit for modeling this data.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Chosen Real-World Scenario:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario: Delivery times for Zomato food orders (in minutes).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Core 3-4 Line Explanation:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Food delivery times on Zomato are well-modeled by a normal distribution because 
the total delivery duration is the composite sum of multiple independent random 
factors—including restaurant kitchen cooking time, packaging, rider dispatch, 
traffic signal stops, and customer hand-off. By the Central Limit Theorem, the sum 
of numerous independent random variables naturally forms a symmetric, unimodal 
bell curve centered around a typical average (e.g., 32 minutes). Extreme deviations 
from this mean—such as an order arriving exceptionally fast in under 12 minutes 
or suffering severe delays beyond 52 minutes—occur symmetrically with rapidly 
diminishing probability in both tails.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Detailed Underlying Statistical Reasons:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Central Limit Theorem (Sum of Independent Additive Components):
   Total Time = Cooking Time + Packing Time + Rider Wait Time + Transit Time + Hand-off Time.
   Even if individual stages have different underlying distributions (e.g., transit 
   delays or kitchen prep), their combined sum inevitably converges to a Gaussian 
   normal distribution.

2. Distinct Central Tendency:
   Most standard restaurant orders in a metropolitan sector are geographically 
   bounded within a 3 to 6 km delivery radius, naturally anchoring delivery times 
   tightly around an expected central mean (e.g., mu = 32 minutes).

3. Symmetric Probability Decay:
   - Super-fast orders (< 15 mins) require every single step to go perfectly 
     without any delay (empty roads, instantaneous prep).
   - Super-slow orders (> 50 mins) require multiple compound bottlenecks 
     (heavy rain, kitchen rush, rider flat tire).
   - Both extreme tail events are equally rare, creating the classic bell curve.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Alternative Real-World Example (Spotify Song Durations):
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For a curated pop playlist on Spotify, song durations also follow an approximate 
normal distribution (mu = 3.2 minutes, sigma = 0.4 minutes). Commercial radio 
standards and streaming algorithm incentives penalize tracks that are excessively 
short (< 1.5 mins) or excessively long (> 5 mins), causing creators to produce 
tracks clustered symmetrically around the 3-minute mean.
