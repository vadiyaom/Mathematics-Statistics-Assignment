TASK 2: Correlation Analysis for Zomato & Swiggy Monthly Orders (6 Friends)
================================================================================

1. Problem Statement:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Given data for 6 friends: their monthly Zomato order count and their monthly 
Swiggy order count, calculate the correlation coefficient between the two lists 
using numpy's corrcoef(). Print the result and explain if the correlation is 
positive, negative, or zero.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Dataset (Monthly Orders for 6 Friends):
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
| Friend               | Monthly Zomato Orders [X] | Monthly Swiggy Orders [Y] |
|:---------------------|:-------------------------:|:-------------------------:|
| Friend 1 (Aarav)     |            12             |            14             |
| Friend 2 (Bhavna)    |            18             |            20             |
| Friend 3 (Chirag)    |             5             |             4             |
| Friend 4 (Divya)     |            22             |            25             |
| Friend 5 (Eshan)     |             8             |             7             |
| Friend 6 (Farhan)    |            15             |            17             |

- Number of friends (n) = 6
- Variable X = Monthly order count on Zomato
- Variable Y = Monthly order count on Swiggy

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Python Implementation (task2_zomato_swiggy.py):
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
```python
import numpy as np

# Monthly order counts for 6 friends
zomato_orders = [12, 18, 5, 22, 8, 15]
swiggy_orders = [14, 20, 4, 25, 7, 17]

# Calculate correlation matrix using numpy's corrcoef()
corr_matrix = np.corrcoef(zomato_orders, swiggy_orders)
r = corr_matrix[0, 1]

print("NumPy Correlation Matrix:")
print(corr_matrix)
print(f"\nPearson Correlation Coefficient (r): {r:.5f}")
```

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Mathematical & Programmatic Output:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Correlation Matrix from `np.corrcoef()`:
   [[1.          0.99607172]
    [0.99607172  1.        ]]

B. Understanding the Matrix Structure:
   - Index [0, 0] = r_xx = Correlation of Zomato with Zomato = 1.0000
   - Index [1, 1] = r_yy = Correlation of Swiggy with Swiggy = 1.0000
   - Index [0, 1] and [1, 0] = r_xy = Correlation between Zomato and Swiggy:
     r = +0.9961 (or 0.99607)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Detailed Explanation (Positive, Negative, or Zero?):
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Mathematical Evaluation:
   - Theoretical Range: -1.0 <= r <= +1.0
   - Positive Correlation : r > 0
   - Negative Correlation : r < 0
   - Zero Correlation     : r = 0 (or r approx 0)

   Because the computed correlation coefficient is r = +0.9961 (> 0), the relationship 
   is a **VERY STRONG POSITIVE CORRELATION**.

B. What This Means Statistically:
   - The two variables move strongly in tandem (co-directional movement).
   - As a friend's monthly order count on Zomato increases, their monthly order 
     count on Swiggy also increases proportionally.
   - For example:
     * High Orders: Divya has the highest Zomato orders (22) and also the highest 
       Swiggy orders (25).
     * Low Orders: Chirag has the lowest Zomato orders (5) and also the lowest 
       Swiggy orders (4).

C. Real-World Behavioral & Economic Context:
   - Overall Propensity for Food Delivery:
     In real-world consumer behavior, people who rely heavily on food delivery 
     (e.g., busy software engineers, college students with demanding schedules, 
     or frequent takeout diners) tend to use BOTH major platforms simultaneously. 
     They cross-shop between Zomato and Swiggy depending on delivery time, 
     active coupon codes, or specific restaurant exclusivity.
   - Conversely, individuals who prefer cooking at home or eating at mess halls 
     maintain an extremely low order volume across all food delivery platforms.
   - Hence, user habit and dining lifestyle generate a distinct positive correlation.

D. What Would Other Outcomes Mean?
   - Negative Correlation (r < 0): 
     Would occur under a strict "Substitution Effect" with a fixed takeout quota. 
     For example, if every friend strictly placed exactly 20 takeout meals per 
     month, ordering more from Zomato would necessarily mean ordering less 
     from Swiggy.
   - Zero Correlation (r approx 0): 
     Would indicate that knowing a person's Zomato ordering habits provides zero 
     predictive power over how many times they order from Swiggy (complete independence).
