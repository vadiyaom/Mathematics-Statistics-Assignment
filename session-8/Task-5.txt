TASK 5: Using Conditional Probability to Improve Recommendations in Flipkart
================================================================================

1. Problem Description:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Explain with a short Python code example how conditional probability could be 
used to improve recommendations in an app like Flipkart (e.g., probability of 
buying headphones given user bought a phone).

Hint: Use counts of users who bought both items vs total who bought a phone.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Mathematical Concept: Conditional Probability P(B | A)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Conditional probability quantifies the likelihood that event B occurs, given 
prior knowledge that event A has already occurred.

Formal Formula:
                             P(A INTERSECT B)      Count(A and B)
         P(B | A) = ---------------------------- = -----------------
                                  P(A)                 Count(A)

Where:
- Event A : User purchases a Smartphone
- Event B : User purchases Wireless Headphones
- (A and B): User purchases both a Smartphone AND Wireless Headphones

Therefore:
                                     Count of users who bought (Phone AND Headphones)
      P(Headphones | Phone) = -------------------------------------------------------------
                                          Total count of users who bought a Phone

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Why Conditional Probability Revolutionizes Recommendations:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Moving Beyond Naive Popularity:
   If an e-commerce platform only looks at raw marginal probabilities P(B), it 
   will recommend the same universally popular items (e.g., groceries, t-shirts, 
   or phone cables) to every visitor regardless of context.

B. Contextual Intent & Basket Analysis (Association Rules):
   When a user adds a Rs. 25,000 smartphone to their cart, their shopping context 
   dramatically shifts. The prior baseline probability of someone buying premium 
   headphones on any random session might only be 28% (P(Headphones) = 0.28). 
   However, conditioned on buying a smartphone, the likelihood jumps to 65% 
   (P(Headphones | Phone) = 0.65). 

C. Lift & Recommendation Ranking:
   The "Lift" is calculated as:
                    P(Headphones | Phone)       0.65
           Lift = ------------------------- = -------- = 2.32x
                       P(Headphones)            0.28
   A phone buyer is 2.32 times more likely to purchase headphones than a random 
   shopper. Flipkart uses this exact metric to rank its "Frequently Bought Together" 
   and "Complete Your Setup" widgets.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. Short Python Code Example (task5_flipkart_recommendation.py):
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
```python
# Flipkart Transaction Data Summary
total_users = 1000
phone_buyers = 400                    # Users who bought a Smartphone (A)
both_phone_and_headphones = 260       # Users who bought BOTH Phone & Headphones (A & B)

# Calculate Conditional Probability: P(Headphones | Phone)
p_headphones_given_phone = both_phone_and_headphones / phone_buyers

# Calculate Unconditional (Baseline) Probability: P(Headphones)
total_headphones_buyers = 280
p_headphones_baseline = total_headphones_buyers / total_users

# Calculate Lift
lift = p_headphones_given_phone / p_headphones_baseline

print(f"Total Phone Buyers                 : {phone_buyers}")
print(f"Bought both Phone and Headphones   : {both_phone_and_headphones}")
print(f"P(Headphones | Phone)              : {p_headphones_given_phone:.2f} ({p_headphones_given_phone * 100:.1f}%)")
print(f"Baseline P(Headphones)             : {p_headphones_baseline:.2f} ({p_headphones_baseline * 100:.1f}%)")
print(f"Recommendation Lift                : {lift:.2f}x higher likelihood")
```

Output:
- Total Phone Buyers                 : 400
- Bought both Phone and Headphones   : 260
- P(Headphones | Phone)              : 0.65 (65.0%)
- Baseline P(Headphones)             : 0.28 (28.0%)
- Recommendation Lift                : 2.32x higher likelihood

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Multi-Product Recommendation Ranking in Flipkart:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
By evaluating conditional probabilities across a catalog of candidate items, 
Flipkart ranks the most relevant suggestions:

| Rank | Candidate Accessory Item | Bought with Phone | P(Item \| Phone) | Recommendation Action |
|:----:|:-------------------------|:-----------------:|:----------------:|:----------------------|
|  #1  | Screen Protector         |        340        |      85.0%       | Show as 1st add-on checkbox on product page |
|  #2  | Phone Case / Cover       |        320        |      80.0%       | Show as 2nd add-on checkbox on product page |
|  #3  | Wireless Headphones      |        260        |      65.0%       | Show in "Frequently Bought Together" bundle |
|  #4  | Power Bank               |        120        |      30.0%       | Show in post-purchase "You Might Also Need" |
|  #5  | Smart Watch              |        90         |      22.5%       | Secondary promotional carousel |
|  #6  | Microwave Oven           |        10         |       2.5%       | Suppressed / Not Recommended |

Business Impact:
1. Higher Conversion Rate (CR) : Showing high-probability accessories converts passive browsing into active cross-sells.
2. Higher Average Order Value (AOV): Prompting a Rs. 1,499 headphone or Rs. 299 screen guard at checkout increases order revenue with minimal customer acquisition friction.
3. Enhanced User Experience (UX): Reduces customer search friction by anticipating what accessories they immediately need.
