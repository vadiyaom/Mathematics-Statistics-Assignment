TASK 2: Simulation of Rolling a Six-Sided Die 100 Times
================================================================================

1. Problem Description:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Simulate rolling a six-sided dice 100 times using Python's random module, 
count how many times a '6' appears, and calculate the experimental probability 
of getting a '6'.

Hint: Use random.randint(1,6) in a loop and keep a counter for '6'.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Theoretical vs. Experimental Probability:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Theoretical Probability P_theoretical(E):
   - Calculated mathematically assuming a perfectly fair, unbiased six-sided die.
   - Sample space: S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}, total outcomes n(S) = 6.
   - Favorable event: Face '6', n(E) = 1.
   - P_theoretical(6) = 1 / 6 = 0.16666... (approx. 16.67%)

B. Experimental (Empirical) Probability P_experimental(E):
   - Calculated from observed results of an actual repeated experiment.
   - Formula:
                                 Number of times event '6' occurred
         P_experimental(6) = -----------------------------------------
                               Total number of dice rolls conducted (N)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Python Implementation (task2_dice_simulation.py):
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
```python
import random

# Initialize simulation parameters
total_rolls = 100
count_6 = 0

# Roll the die 100 times
for _ in range(total_rolls):
    roll = random.randint(1, 6)
    if roll == 6:
        count_6 += 1

# Calculate probabilities
experimental_probability = count_6 / total_rolls
theoretical_probability = 1 / 6

print(f"Total Rolls conducted           : {total_rolls}")
print(f"Number of times '6' appeared    : {count_6}")
print(f"Experimental Probability of '6' : {experimental_probability:.4f} ({experimental_probability * 100:.2f}%)")
print(f"Theoretical Probability of '6'  : {theoretical_probability:.4f} ({theoretical_probability * 100:.2f}%)")
```

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Law of Large Numbers (LLN) Explanation:
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why does the experimental probability (20.00%) deviate slightly from the theoretical 
probability (16.67%)?

1. Finite Sample Variation:
   In a sample of only 100 rolls, random sampling fluctuations naturally cause 
   slight differences between observed counts and theoretical expectations. 
   Each roll is completely independent of all previous rolls.

2. Law of Large Numbers:
   As the number of repetitions (N) increases toward infinity, the experimental 
   probability is mathematically guaranteed to converge toward the true theoretical 
   probability:
   - At N = 100 trials    : P_exp(6) approx 0.1800 (Diff = 0.0133)
   - At N = 1,000 trials  : P_exp(6) approx 0.1670 (Diff = 0.0003)
   - At N = 100,000 trials: P_exp(6) approx 0.1675 (Diff = 0.0009)
